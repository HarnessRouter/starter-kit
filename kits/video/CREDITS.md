# Credits

## Templates and skill

The five worked storyboards in `templates/templates.json` — their shot lists, their prompts and
their arrangement — are original to this kit, and so is the guidance in
`skills/video-storyboard/`. What is not original is the craft underneath: which shots a short film
needs, in what order, and why a face has to be established once rather than described repeatedly.
These are the sources that settled those questions.

**Shot grammar — what a shot is, and which ones a sequence needs**

- Daniel Arijon, *Grammar of the Film Language* (Silman-James Press) — the standard catalogue of
  shot relationships. The reveal → detail → human-scale → proof order in the product template is an
  application of its sequence patterns, not an invention.
- Steven D. Katz, *Film Directing Shot by Shot* (Michael Wiese Productions) — storyboarding as
  planning rather than illustration, and the discipline of one action per shot. The rule that a
  shot description naming four things is four shots comes from here.
- Bruce Block, *The Visual Story* (Focal Press) — visual continuity across a cut: tone, colour,
  movement and depth as things that must be held deliberately. This is why `Style:` and `Palette:`
  are two global lines repeated verbatim into every prompt rather than re-described per shot.

**Cutting, and how long a shot holds**

- Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye* (Silman-James Press) — what a cut is for, and the priority
  order that puts emotion and story above spatial continuity. The templates' preference for holding
  a shot a beat longer than is comfortable, rather than adding shots, follows it.

**Continuity as a job, not an intention**

- Pat P. Miller, *Script Supervising and Film Continuity* (Focal Press) — continuity is somebody's
  explicit responsibility on a real set, tracked shot by shot, precisely because it cannot be held
  in anyone's head. `skills/video-storyboard/references/continuity.md` is that idea applied to a
  generative pipeline, where the failure mode is sharper: a described character is a distribution
  of faces, and re-describing draws a new one each time.

The storyboards themselves are written against products and stories that are ours and imaginary.

## Canvas

The canvas is **[Excalidraw](https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw)** (MIT). Dragging, resizing,
freehand drawing, multi-select, frames and the scene file format are all Excalidraw's; this kit adds
media elements and one extra top-level `timeline` key. `scene.excalidraw` is a real Excalidraw scene
and opens in any Excalidraw unchanged.

Excalidraw's hand-drawn fonts — Excalifont, Nunito and Virgil — ship with that package under their
own licenses and are staged into the app's build rather than fetched from a CDN, so a deployment
with no outbound network still renders text.

## Components

The conversation surface, the composer, the carousel, the dialogs, the resizable panes and the
HarnessRouter transport come from **[reifyui](https://www.npmjs.com/package/reifyui)** (MIT), which
is where they live so that a fourth kit does not start a fourth copy of them.

## Assembly

Export trims, scales, letterboxes, concatenates and mixes with **[FFmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/)**,
and measures its own output with `ffprobe`. FFmpeg is **not bundled with this kit**: it is installed
into the HarnessRouter image from the base distribution's packages, and those builds are typically
GPL-licensed rather than LGPL. A production distributor is responsible for confirming the license of
the FFmpeg build actually included in its image and shipping the notices that build requires. Where
FFmpeg is absent the kit reports export unavailable rather than degrading.

## Generation providers

Clips, stills and speech are generated by third-party models reached through the provider this
deployment has connected. Those models, their outputs and their terms are the provider's, not this
kit's — including what may be done commercially with generated material, and any watermark a model
applies. The kit names the model that actually produced each piece of media, in the element's own
metadata, so that question can always be answered after the fact.

No provider credential is stored in this repository, in a kit, or in any document a kit writes.

## Software dependencies

The Videos app installs third-party packages from npm, including React, ReifyUI, Excalidraw, Lucide
and their transitive dependencies. Those packages remain governed by their own licenses.
`package.json` and `package-lock.json` identify the installed package versions; a production
distributor must generate and ship the notices required for the actual dependency set included in
its build.
